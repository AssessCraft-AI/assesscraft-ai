# Academic Quality Assurance Manual (Examinations & Assessments)

**Document Reference:** QA-EXAM-POL-2024  
**Issuing Authority:** University Academic Standards Board  
**Scope:** All Undergraduate and Postgraduate Taught Programs  

## Section 4: Examination Paper Construction & Equivalence Standards

### 4.1 Assessment Blueprinting
All summative written examinations must be drafted according to a validated Assessment Blueprint matrix mapping:
- Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs);
- Bloom’s Revised Taxonomy cognitive domains (Remembering to Creating);
- Curricular Topics;
- Item types and assigned mark allocations.

Every Course Learning Outcome specified in the approved course specification must receive a non-zero mark allocation in the final summative examination.

### 4.2 Repeat and Supplementary Examination Equivalence
Where a module provides a Main examination sitting and a subsequent Repeat / Supplementary examination sitting:
1. **Structural Parity:** The repeat examination paper must exhibit 100% structural equivalence with the main examination paper in total marks, duration, question format proportions, and CLO mark weighting.
2. **Cognitive Balance:** The distribution of marks across Bloom's cognitive taxonomy levels must match between Main and Repeat papers with an absolute difference of zero percentage points across guaranteed bands.
3. **Item Non-Duplication (Zero Reuse Rule):** Under no circumstances may an individual question or item used in the Main sitting be reused in the Repeat sitting of the same academic session.
4. **Difficulty Variance:** Estimated average item difficulty between papers must not diverge by more than 5% (0.25 difficulty points on a 1-5 scale).
5. **Disclaimer:** While structural blueprint parity is formally guaranteed by institutional constraint validation, empirical equivalence requires post-hoc statistical analysis of student response data.

### 4.3 External Moderation Protocols
1. The External Moderator must review both Version A (Main) and Version B (Repeat) concurrently alongside complete model solutions and grading rubrics.
2. The Moderator must verify that all flags raised during internal review (including ambiguity flags, key discrepancies, and similarity warnings) have been resolved.
3. Formal approval is granted via digital sign-off before examination papers are transmitted to the secure printing registry.
